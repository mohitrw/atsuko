PART 1: THE PHONETIC SCIENCE OF "NX" vs "ANEX"
================================================================================

Why do "NX" and "ANEX" sound identical when spoken?
Look at how English speakers pronounce letters:
- The letter "N" is pronounced: /ɛn/ ("en")
- The letter "X" is pronounced: /ɛks/ ("ex")
When you say "N - X", your vocal cords produce:
  /ɛn/ + /ɛks/ = [ɛn.ɛks] or [æn.ɛks] ("ANN-eks" / "ANEX")

In standard English, the dictionary word for this sound is:
  "ANNEX" (/ˈæn.ɛks/ or /əˈnɛks/)

When you say "NX" or "Anex" to Google's Speech Recognition engine:
- Google's English AI hears [æn.ɛks].
- Because "NX" is an acronym, Google STT matches it directly to the valid
  English dictionary word: "annex" (or "anex", "nx", "n x").
